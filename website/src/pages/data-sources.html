Data sources

Public data, cited and bundled.

Headroom's comparisons use official U.S. public datasets, shipped inside the app and read-only. Here's what they are, their vintage, and what they mean.

The datasets

WhatSourceVintageDefinition
Rent & income (by county)U.S. Census Bureau — American Community Survey (5-year)2019–2023Median gross rent and median household income for ~3,144 counties.
Energy (by state)U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA)2024Average monthly residential electricity bill.
Food (by income band)U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Consumer Expenditure Survey2023Average annual food spending by income band; the U.S. average is ~$9,985/yr.

How we use them

  • National rent uses the official published ACS figure ($1,348/month), not an unweighted average of county medians (which understates the real typical).
  • Food has no county or state dimension in the data, so it's compared to a typical for your income band and to the U.S. average.
  • Averages and medians are exactly that — broad references. Real costs vary widely, so Headroom treats them as context, never a target.

Datasets are validated on every change (required columns, numeric ranges, unique county codes) and updated through a documented process, not manual spreadsheet edits.
