Common questions

What does the “where your money goes furthest” part do?

It ranks U.S. states and counties by how much you'd have left each month living there — running your own numbers against each place's real typical rent (Census) and utilities (EIA). You can rank by your current pay or by a chosen job's local pay across 116 occupations (BLS wage data). It's for perspective, never a nudge to move. More on how it works.

Is it really private?

Yes. The numbers you enter are stored only on your device and are never uploaded. There's no server for your financial data, no analytics, and no tracking — it works fully offline, even in Airplane Mode. More on privacy.

Do I have to connect my bank?

No — and you can't. DebtShield never links to a bank or credit account. You type a few numbers yourself, which keeps it private and simple.

Where do the rent, utilities, and pay numbers come from?

Public U.S. data, bundled inside the app: typical rent from the Census Bureau, energy from the EIA, and occupation wages from the BLS. They're typical figures for perspective, never a target or a guarantee. See the sources.

Is it telling me to move?

No. DebtShield shows where your money would stretch further as perspective — the trade-offs of a move (family, jobs, community) are yours alone. It never ranks a place as “better” for your life, only how the dollars would compare.

Does the “Ask” feature use AI that sees my data?

No. Ask is deterministic — it computes every figure from your own numbers on your device and never sends anything to a server or guesses. It won't invent numbers, and it points you to free help for anything that needs a real person.

Is this financial advice?

No. DebtShield is educational information about your own money, with cited public data — not financial, legal, tax, or housing advice, and it doesn't promise any outcome. For decisions, it points you to free, reputable help like 211 and HUD-approved counselors.
