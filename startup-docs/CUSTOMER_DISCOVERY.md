# Customer Discovery

_Structured questions to validate demand — with individuals and with
institutions — before building more or setting prices. Learn, don't pitch._

## Hypotheses to test
- H1: Students/young adults want a private money view and distrust bank-linking.
- H2: They'll return a second month if the first is genuinely useful.
- H3: Colleges/nonprofits want a financial-wellness tool that doesn't expose or
  surveil participants, and will run a pilot.
- H4: An institution will pay for services (deployment, training, aggregate
  reporting), not for data.

## Individual interviews (see `../docs/USER_INTERVIEW_GUIDE.md`)
Focus on how they track money now, what's stressful, trust in on-device privacy,
and whether the year-ahead helps or overwhelms. Never collect their real figures.

## Institution interviews — questions
- What financial-wellness tools do you use now? What frustrates you?
- How do you think about participants' privacy and data liability?
- Who owns this decision and what's your budget cycle?
- What would a successful pilot look like to you? How would you measure it?
- Would aggregate, voluntary outcomes be enough to justify it internally?
- What would stop you from running a pilot this term?

## Signals we're looking for
- Unprompted mention of privacy/data-liability as a pain.
- A named owner + budget + timeline (not just interest).
- Willingness to introduce us to a second stakeholder.

## Kill criteria
- If institutions consistently want to see individual data, that conflicts with
  the mission — reframe or walk, don't compromise privacy.
- If individuals don't return a second month in the beta, fix the product before
  scaling sales.

## Recording
Notes without any personal financial figures. Track hypotheses as
supported/challenged, with quotes. Feed into `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md` and
`PRICING_EXPERIMENTS.md`.
