# Competitive Positioning

_Honest positioning. Describe others fairly; compete on a real, defensible wedge._

## Landscape (categories)
- **Bank-linking budgeters** (e.g. YNAB, Copilot, Rocket Money, Monarch): powerful
  automation, but require linking accounts and often carry subscriptions or
  upsells. Privacy and trust cost; excludes the bank-averse.
- **Credit-score apps**: gamify a score and monetize offers (cards, loans).
  Misaligned with someone trying to avoid debt.
- **Spreadsheets / notes**: private and flexible, but manual and unclear about
  "where am I heading?".

## DebtShield's wedge
- **On-device privacy, no bank connection, no ads, no upsell.** Nothing leaves
  the phone. This is the core differentiator and the reason a distrustful or
  underserved user will try it.
- **Plain-dollar, non-shaming** framing (no score/grade), with a **transparent
  year-ahead** projection that shows its assumptions.
- **B2B2C for the underserved** (colleges/nonprofits) — a channel the
  subscription apps largely ignore, where privacy is a buying criterion.

## Honest tradeoffs (say these plainly)
- No automatic transaction import — the user types their numbers. Simpler and
  private, but more manual than bank-linking apps.
- Benchmarks are broad public averages, not personalized bank data.
- Not a full budgeting/envelopes system by design — it answers one question well.

## Messaging do/don't
- Do: "Private by design. No bank connection. Your numbers stay on your phone."
- Don't: disparage competitors, or claim features we don't have.
- Don't: imply we prevent debt or guarantee outcomes.
