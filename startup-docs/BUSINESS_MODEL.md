# Business Model

_How DebtShield can fund itself **ethically** without selling personal data or
compromising the on-device privacy promise._

## Shape: B2B2C
The app is free/low-friction for individuals. Institutions sponsor access,
education, and deployment for the people they serve. An institution **never**
receives individual financial entries or verdicts.

## Who pays
Colleges, community nonprofits, workforce/re-entry programs, employers
(financial-wellness benefit), and housing organizations.

## What they pay for (services, not data)
- Deployment support and staff/facilitator training.
- Educational implementation materials and co-branded educational pages.
- **Aggregate, voluntary** outcome reporting (survey-based; never individual).
- Annual benchmark data updates and methodology notes.
- Accessibility documentation and priority support.
- Partner implementation assistance.

## What we will never do
- Sell or share personal financial data.
- Let an institution view individual profiles or verdicts.
- Add ads, loan/credit referrals, or debt-settlement monetization.

## Cost structure (qualitative)
- Apple Developer Program fee; static website hosting (low).
- Engineering + support time (the main cost).
- Annual data-refresh effort.
- No per-user server cost for financial data (there is none).

## Value proposition per buyer
- **Colleges/nonprofits:** a private tool participants actually trust; supports
  financial-wellness goals without surveillance.
- **Employers:** a benefit that doesn't spy on employees' finances.
- **All:** strong privacy story, honest methodology, accessibility.

## Sequencing
Free beta → paid pilots (services) → annual institutional licenses. Payments are
**not** activated until a real customer needs them (see `../STARTUP_ROADMAP.md`
Phase 15). Legal agreements require attorney review.
